# Methods and source attribution

## Observation snapshot

The main view contains 9,575,922 records for 2024, retrieved through the Atlas of Living Australia (ALA) on 10 September 2026. Sources are eBird Australia (`dr2009`), BirdLife Australia Birdata (`dr359`) and iNaturalist Australia (`dr1411`). These are aggregated database records, not estimated individuals or unique observers.

Exact request URLs and SHA-256 checksums are stored in [the query log](../data/provenance.json). Each query uses `pageSize=0` and a sufficiently large facet limit to obtain exhaustive counts, not a first-page occurrence sample. The preparation script checks that state, monthly, grid, family and source totals reconcile.

Filters: `class:Aves`; country Australia; human observation; occurrence status present; spatially valid; month 1–12; longitude 112–154°E; latitude 44–10°S; one of the eight main states/territories; and the selected source and year. The spatial window focuses on mainland Australia, Tasmania and nearby islands/waters. It is not every external Australian territory. ALA quality flags are useful but do not guarantee every observation is correct. Sources are combined without cross-platform deduplication.

There are 760 named species in the retained 2024 records and 217,108 records without a species-level assignment. These unassigned records remain in denominators for overall monthly totals, but cannot enter named-species ranks. This is not an inventory of native Australian species.

## Transformations

- Map grid: ALA `point-1` facets, whose coordinates are rounded to whole degrees. The displayed cells have equal angular dimensions, not equal physical area: a cell at 44°S covers about a quarter less ground than one at 10°S, so the equal-size-bin assumption of the Week 8 bin-map notes holds only approximately. Colour shows raw record counts, not counts per km². Cells are ranked from busiest down with the running share of all records before each; the control (25%, 50%, 75% or 90%) outlines the fewest cells whose combined records reach the chosen share (3, 12, 46 and 106 cells). Each 1° box is clipped to the coastline of the eight states and territories in `scripts/prepare_data.py`, so no square paints sea. 720 of the 814 boxes contain land and are drawn; the other 94 (0.16% of records, mostly pelagic and boat-based trips) are not drawn, though their records still count toward the shares. Every outlined cell contains land. The outline of each share is precomputed as the boundary of the union of its clipped cells, so adjacent cells form one region and a large selection does not become a mesh. There is no "all" option: every cell holds all records, which says nothing. Unselected cells keep their full colour: fading them would change lightness, the channel that carries the count.
- Record density: counts divided by the published total land area of each state, or by the ABS estimated resident population at June 2024, multiplied by 1,000. Coastal records can be included in counts; this is a broad administrative-area comparison. Colour is each state's log-scaled position between fixed limits for the chosen measure (200–150,000 per 1,000 km²; 200–1,400 per 1,000 residents); the printed value carries the number.
- Seasonal clock: `(monthly count / days in month) / (state annual count / 366)`. The neutral value is 1. The diverging colour scale is logarithmic over 0.5–2, so half and double the usual pace sit equally far from the midpoint. Values beyond that domain are clamped, but tooltips retain actual values.
- Bump chart: ranks among all named species within each month; seven displayed species are selected by annual totals. Ties sort by scientific name.
- Bee-eater map and ridgelines: records for `Merops ornatus`. The map sums the one-degree points into two-degree squares, about 120 per season instead of 250, so the circles do not overplot; it draws the chosen season filled and the opposite season as rings, on one fixed area scale. Because each `point-1` coordinate stands for the cell ±0.5° around a whole degree, each circle is drawn at the record-weighted mean of its points rather than at a square's corner-aligned centre, which had put coastal circles about 0.5° out to sea. Ridgeline ticks mark each month's median latitude. Summer is December, January and February within calendar 2024; other seasons follow the standard calendar groups. Latitude distributions are normalised independently within each month and use rounded one-degree bins. At-or-south-of-30°S comparisons therefore have an approximate spatial threshold: they count points rounded to 30°S or further south, from about 29.5°S. Counting only from 30.5°S gives 46.3% in summer (not 48.3%) and 0.7% in winter (not 1.0%).
- Species heatmap: `(species monthly records / all monthly records) / (species annual records / all annual records)`. This controls overall record volume, not observation effort, checklist completeness or species detectability.
- Conservation beeswarm: exact two-part scientific-name matches between the DCCEEW species-level listings and ALA named species, with positive counts. Listed subspecies are not assigned to their parent species. There are 65 matches. Ten in-scope species-level names do not match; this can reflect taxonomy, geographic scope or no observations and is not converted to zero. Dots are packed per status row without overlap and sized with the chart width; the most-recorded bird (Eastern Curlew, Critically Endangered, 9,373 records) and the least-recorded (White-throated Grasswren, Vulnerable, 2) are labelled because they are the two ends of the headline.
- UpSet: exact combinations of positive state associations in the DCCEEW list. Extinct entries and taxa associated only with external jurisdictions are excluded. The remaining 150 extant threatened taxa include species and subspecies. The twelve most common combinations contain 104 taxa; 42 combinations exist.
- Seasonal contour map: summer (Dec, Jan, Feb) and winter (Jun, Jul, Aug) 2024 `point-1` counts, for all records and for the bee-eater, are each spread with a Gaussian kernel (σ = 1°) onto a 0.25° lattice and converted to shares of that season's national total. The mapped value is log2(winter share / summer share), clipped to ±3 and contoured at a constant interval of 1 (−2, −1, 0, 1, 2), so each band is a doubling of the ratio. The zero line, equal shares, is drawn as the index contour. Lattice points off the mainland and Tasmania, or with too little smoothed evidence (400 records for all birds, 12 for the bee-eater), are left empty and shown grey with their own legend entry. Contours are computed with contourpy in `scripts/prepare_data.py`; the page draws them with Vega-Lite. The surface is an estimate, not an observation at every point.
- Dorling cartogram: one circle per jurisdiction, starting at its projected label point and moved apart by a collision force until no two overlap. Circle area is proportional to the number of listed threatened taxa associated with the jurisdiction; fill lightness (ColorBrewer Greens) shows its 2024 records. Taxa listed in several jurisdictions count in each. Circles may settle south of the coast (Tasmania is pushed south by Victoria); they are not clamped back, which would re-create overlaps. Selecting a state highlights the UpSet combinations that include it.

## Why 2024?

The accessible ALA eBird snapshot contains January–August 2025 but no September–December 2025 records or 2026 records under these filters. eBird supplies 86.8% of 2024 records, so a 2025 calendar missing four eBird months would distort every seasonal pattern. Every chart therefore uses 2024, the latest complete year; the page states this in Methods. `scripts/check.mjs` asserts the eBird gap, so a refreshed snapshot that fills it fails the check and prompts a review. Even a year with twelve reported months need not contain every observation ever made.
