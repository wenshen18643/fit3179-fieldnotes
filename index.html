2The seasons

Turn the calendar.
The cast changes.

October is the busiest page.

Nationally, October averages 37,847 records a day, the highest of 2024. The tropical Northern Territory runs to its own clock: busiest in the dry season, June to October.

3The blind spots

Often recorded
doesn’t mean safe.

Read with care

A record shows a bird was there. It does not show it is recovering.

Vulnerable, Endangered and Critically Endangered mark rising risk of extinction.

Who is threatened

56% of threatened-bird records are shorebirds and wetland birds.

Most are migratory waders feeding on tidal flats. Albatrosses and petrels are mostly seen from boats.

How to read the evidence

The same bird can be logged on more than one platform, and busy places are logged more often.

A threatened bird’s current listing says nothing about its numbers in 2024. Species whose names did not match exactly are left out.

Methods and limitations

9,575,922 records cover 760 named species; 217,108 more have no species name and count only in totals. Monthly shares divide a bird’s records by all records that month; the seasonal clock divides by days in the month and compares each state with its own average.

The seasonal contour map spreads each season’s 1° counts with a Gaussian kernel (σ = 1°), converts them to shares of that season’s national total, and contours the log ratio of winter to summer share on a 0.25° lattice, clipped to land. Places with too little smoothed evidence stay blank.

Threatened-species matching uses the DCCEEW scientific name at species level only: 65 species match with 2024 records. The spike map divides their records by all records in each 1° square and omits squares with fewer than 300 records. Kinds of bird follow family: albatrosses and petrels; shorebirds, terns, herons and painted-snipe; all others.

Density uses Geoscience Australia land area and ABS June 2024 residents. 1° squares shrink slightly towards the south, so the atlas shows counts, not density. The latitude profiles use 1° bins, so 30°S is approximate. The year-to-year chart includes every featured bird with at least 500 iNaturalist records in 2024.
